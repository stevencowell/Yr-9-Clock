Weeks 13-14: Oil finish and clock mechanism fit-up

Plan and inspect the three-coat oil system, then fit and test the supplied battery mechanism.

Theory 1

Planning the three-coat oil finish

A three-coat oil finish should be treated as a planned sequence, not as one long finishing session. Each coat must be applied under teacher direction using the approved product information. The aim is to build a consistent protective finish while keeping the Clock clean, controlling contamination and recording evidence of each stage. Rushing a coat or guessing when to continue can leave uneven areas, trapped dust or a surface that is not ready for the next step.

Before finishing begins, inspect the Clock under suitable light. The timber should meet the approved surface-preparation standard, with remaining scratches, dents, glue marks and uneven sanding identified before oil is applied. Oil can make faults more visible rather than hiding them. Confirm that the hidden drawer and any parts that must remain separate are organised according to the teacher-approved finishing plan.

The finishing area must be prepared before the product is opened. Follow teacher direction and the relevant school procedures for ventilation, personal protective equipment, bench protection, fire safety and access control. Remove unnecessary tools, dust and loose materials from the area. The approved oil, application materials and waste container must be ready so the work does not need to be left unattended while equipment is collected.

Read the product label and Safety Data Sheet with your teacher. Important checkpoints include required surface preparation, application method, ventilation, personal protective equipment, drying or recoating conditions, storage, first aid and disposal of used materials. Do not assume that instructions for another oil product are suitable. Any between-coat surface preparation must also follow the product information and teacher direction.

  1. Before Coat 1: photograph the prepared surface and record the approved product and relevant instructions.
  2. After Coat 1: record the date, coat number, observations and any teacher-approved correction.
  3. Before Coat 2: confirm that the surface meets the stated recoating conditions and has been inspected by the teacher.
  4. After Coat 2: check for consistent coverage, contamination, rough areas, runs or missed sections without handling the surface unnecessarily.
  5. Before and after Coat 3: repeat the approved checks and record final evidence of the completed finish.

Readiness for the next coat is judged from the product information, the condition of the surface and teacher approval, not simply because a lesson has ended or a certain amount of time seems to have passed. The final coat must also be allowed to reach the required condition before the Clock is handled, assembled further, photographed or evaluated.

Key idea: A reliable three-coat finish comes from repeating the same approved preparation, application, inspection and recording process for every coat.

Oil finish safety, application and between-coat quality

An oil finish can improve the appearance of the Clock and protect its prepared timber surfaces, but it must be treated as a chemical product rather than simply “painted on”. Before work begins, read the selected product label and Safety Data Sheet (SDS) with your teacher. These sources identify the required ventilation, personal protective equipment (PPE), first-aid information, drying and recoating conditions, handling precautions and disposal method. Do not rely on instructions from a different oil product because products can have different hazards and requirements.

The finishing area must be prepared according to teacher direction and the school’s procedures. Suitable ventilation and the correct PPE must be in place before the container is opened. The Clock, work surface and approved application materials should be organised so the finish can be applied without rushing, reaching across wet surfaces or moving through a cluttered area. Dust, loose fibres and contamination can become trapped in the coating, so inspect the prepared Clock before starting.

Each coat should form thin, even coverage over the surfaces identified in the teacher-approved finishing plan. Work methodically so that edges, corners and less visible areas are not missed. Too much product in one place can create runs, patches or slow-drying areas, while uneven coverage can produce an inconsistent colour or sheen. Follow the product information and teacher demonstration for application, removal of excess product and treatment of areas such as the hidden drawer and its opening. Do not allow finish to interfere with the required fit or movement of components.

Use this between-coat inspection process:

  1. Record the coat number, date and the relevant product-information checkpoint.
  2. Allow the coat to dry or cure for the stated period under the required conditions.
  3. Inspect under suitable light for missed areas, runs, pooled finish, dust, roughness or uneven appearance.
  4. Check that the hidden drawer and other fitted parts remain aligned and functional, as directed by the teacher.
  5. Photograph useful evidence and record any fault, likely cause and approved correction.
  6. Begin the next stage only when the label, SDS and teacher direction confirm that the surface is ready.

Oil-contaminated cloths, applicators and other waste may present a serious hazard. Never leave them crumpled on a bench, place them in an ordinary bin or take them from the workshop. Handle and dispose of all contaminated materials exactly as stated on the product information and by the teacher.

Key idea: A successful three-coat finish depends on following the actual product instructions, applying controlled coverage and using recorded evidence to decide when each coat is safe and ready for the next stage.

Accurate fit-up of the battery clock mechanism

Fitting the battery clock mechanism is a precision stage, not a force-fit task. The aim is to connect the completed timber body with the supplied mechanism so the Clock is secure, correctly aligned and able to operate without interference. Before fitting anything, compare the finished opening and surrounding surfaces with the supplied mechanism information and the teacher-approved project drawing. Because mechanism designs vary, all requirements for the shaft, face, fixing parts, battery and clearances must come from the supplied information and teacher direction.

Protect the finished timber while handling and testing the mechanism. Place the Clock on the approved clean support and keep loose hardware, batteries and other parts organised so they cannot scratch the surface or become lost. Check the opening for remaining dust, finish build-up or damage. Do not scrape, enlarge or alter the opening unless the teacher has inspected the problem and approved a correction. A part that does not fit easily may be the wrong way around, misaligned or incompatible with the completed opening. Forcing it can damage the finish, split timber or bend mechanism components.

Use the following fit-up process under teacher direction:

  1. Identify the parts. Check the supplied mechanism information and confirm the correct orientation and fitting order.
  2. Inspect the opening. Look for uneven edges, finish build-up, dust or other conditions that could affect seating.
  3. Trial the fit gently. Support the timber body and mechanism while checking that the parts enter and sit correctly without force.
  4. Check alignment. View the Clock from the front and confirm that the display and visible parts are positioned as intended.
  5. Confirm clearance. Check that moving parts can operate without touching the timber, drawer or other project components.
  6. Secure and test. Use only the supplied fixing parts and teacher-approved method, then test the function as directed.

Functional testing should be careful and evidence-based. Observe whether the display remains aligned, the mechanism stays secure and the moving parts operate freely. Listen and look for rubbing, hesitation, looseness or movement of the mechanism within the Clock. If a fault appears, stop and report the exact symptom instead of guessing or attempting a repair. Record the fit-up with an appropriate photograph and a brief folio note explaining what was checked and how correct function was confirmed.

Key idea: Accurate fit-up comes from checking compatibility, alignment, clearance and function against the supplied mechanism information—not from forcing parts or making unapproved alterations.
